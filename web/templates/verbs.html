How verbs change

A Skiri verb is built around a stem. Pieces before and after the stem say who does it, how many, and how the statement is meant, so one word often does the work of an English sentence. The dictionary shows this in two places: five forms under every verb, and seven verbs written out in full.

1. The five forms under every verb

The dictionary lists a verb under its stem. A stem is never a word by itself: nobody says at for “go”. So under each verb the dictionary prints up to five whole words that people do say. Learn what the five are and you can read any verb entry. Choose a verb:

4. Stems that change with the number of people

Nine common verbs use a different stem when two people, or more than two, do the thing. “Go” is at for one person and war for two.

What this page is, and is not

Every Skiri form here is printed in the dictionary. This site does not conjugate verbs: a program that tried was right for too few forms and was taken out. To find a form of another verb, look at its five forms in the dictionary and at the seven verbs here, and ask a speaker or a teacher. No speaker of Pawnee has reviewed this page.
