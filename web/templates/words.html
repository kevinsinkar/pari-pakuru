Word building

How Skiri words are built

Many Skiri words are made of smaller pieces, and the dictionary prints the pieces for most of its words. Learn to read them and one known piece opens up dozens of words.

Noun endings Reading a derivation Words that share a piece See them as a picture The labels

1. Start with five pieces that make nouns

Parks lists five pieces that are added to make nouns, each with a word taken apart. The first, -uʔ, ends hundreds of nouns: it marks a noun that stands on its own, and it has no translation.

2. Read what the dictionary prints

After the meaning of a word the dictionary gives its derivation in angled brackets: first the pieces, then a literal translation. Here are six words as it prints them. Choose any piece to see the other words that have it.

  • + A plus sign stands between the main pieces of the word.
  • . - A dot or a hyphen inside a piece joins the smaller parts of one stem.
  • asaa- A dash at the end marks a piece that never stands alone as a word.
  • (ut...) A note like this after a verb stem names the small piece (the preverb: ut, ir or uur) that the stem needs. In a word it comes before the stem.
  • NOM Capital letters are grammatical labels. They are listed below.
  • , … The literal translation follows the comma. Its words are not always in the order of the pieces.

3. Find the words that share a piece

Type a piece, or an English word, or choose one of the pieces the dictionary prints most often. Reading a list of words side by side is how the meaning of a piece becomes clear. The word web draws the same words as a picture, each joined to its pieces.

What this page is, and is not

Every piece and every literal translation here is printed in the dictionary; the page only cuts a derivation at its plus signs and lists together the words in which the same piece is printed. It does not say which word of a translation belongs to which piece. Where a piece is spelled like a dictionary entry, the entries are listed, but several entries often share a spelling and the dictionary does not say which one a piece is.

Words to do with ceremony are left out until the Pawnee community has reviewed them. No speaker of Pawnee has reviewed this page.
