About Pâri Pakûru'

This site is a study aid for the Skiri Pawnee language. It puts two printed books into a form you can search and study: a dictionary and a textbook. Every Skiri word and form you read here comes from those books. Nothing has been made up, and no form is shown that a program worked out by itself.

The two books

  • Douglas R. Parks and Lula Nora Pratt, A Dictionary of Skiri Pawnee (Lincoln: University of Nebraska Press, 2008). The dictionary entries on this site are the Skiri-to-English entries of that book: about 4,200 of them.
  • Pawnee Cultural Retention Committee, Pari Pakuru' (1979), the textbook often called the Blue Book. Its twenty lessons are here, and 93 entries made from words of the textbook. Each is headed in the dictionary's letters, with the form the textbook prints beside it where the project has it, and has no phonetic form, because the textbook gives none. More than a third of them are words the dictionary has too: the textbook does not write long vowels, so the same word looks different there. For those, the dictionary's own entry is named.

The books belong to their authors and publishers. The language belongs to the Pawnee people.

What you can do here

  • Search the dictionary in Skiri or in English. A result shows the word, how to say it, its class, its meanings and the page of the dictionary it stands on. More from this entry opens the rest of what the dictionary prints: examples, the numbered verb forms, other forms of the word, the way Parks takes the word apart, and the forms he cites from related languages.
  • Read the twenty lessons of the textbook, with their dialogues and word lists.
  • Practise with flashcards: weekly sets of everyday words. Your progress is kept in your own browser, not on a server.
  • Learn the sounds on the Sounds page, and take any word syllable by syllable: its phonetic form, as the dictionary prints it, is cut at the syllable dots, and each sound is explained.
  • See how words are built on the Word building page. The dictionary prints the pieces of most words; the page shows them, and lists together the words in which the same piece is printed.
  • See how verbs change on the Verbs page: the five forms the dictionary gives for every verb, and the seven verbs it writes out in full.
  • Play short practice games over the flashcard words, the seven verbs and the pieces of words.
  • Go through the words by topic, or follow the six steps of Start here.

These pages arrange what the books print; they add no word and no form. What they add is plain English: the comparisons with English sounds, the names of the persons of a verb, the short explanations. Those are this site's, and no speaker has checked them.

How a word is written

  • The large spelling is a learner spelling, with a circumflex for a long vowel (â) and an apostrophe for the glottal stop.
  • Beside it is the spelling of the dictionary itself.
  • The form in square brackets is Parks's phonetic form, in his own symbols, exactly as the dictionary prints it.
  • The line in plain letters with hyphens is a rough guide to saying the word, made from the phonetic form by fixed rules. Capitals mark the accented syllable. It is a help, not a second source: where it and the phonetic form disagree, the phonetic form is right.

How far to trust it

  • The dictionary entries have been compared with the text of the printed pages, value by value, most recently in November 2026. A short list of small differences is left, and each is recorded in the project's files.
  • The textbook was read from a scan. Its accent marks and raised dots were restored by reading the scan a second time; a few dozen forms are still in doubt.
  • No speaker of Pawnee and no one from the Pawnee Nation has yet reviewed this site. It was put together from the two books with computer tools. Treat it as a way into the books, and trust speakers and teachers before it.
  • Words to do with ceremony are waiting for review by the Pawnee community. Until then the flashcard set for them is not shown, and their entries show the meanings and examples only.

If you find a mistake

Every search result has a link, Send a correction. It opens a form on GitHub, where the project is kept, with the word already filled in; you need a free GitHub account to send it. You can also write a note about anything else on the site. Say what is wrong and, if you can, what the book has on which page.

What is not on this site

The project also has a program that runs on a single computer, with a page for every entry, a sentence builder and study tools. Parts of it work forms out by rule, and those are not reliable enough to publish, so they are kept off this site. The program, the data and a record of every check are in the project's repository.
