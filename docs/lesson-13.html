KIRU RATSUKS•AT?

Lesson 13 — Where Did You Go?

Blue Book pp. 85–90

Why do the spellings differ?

The same Pawnee word appears in several writing systems, and they are all telling you the same thing:

  • Blue Book — the practical spelling printed in Pari Pakuru' (1979), exactly as the Pawnee Cultural Retention Committee wrote it. Some letters follow English habits (for example d for the soft Pawnee r).
  • Dictionary entry — the learner spelling from the Parks Dictionary, with a circumflex (â) marking long vowels.
  • Say it — the simplified pronunciation. UPPERCASE syllables carry high pitch, which changes meaning in Pawnee.
  • IPA — the exact scholarly transcription recorded by Douglas Parks from fluent speakers. When the other spellings disagree, the IPA is the authority.

Grammar & Notes

Adverbs

Raku•kariu' and tsuskiit are adverbs and go directly before the verb. They are tied to the verb, not to the object noun as would be an adjective like tawit, "three." Tawit kaas tatutak•erit. I saw three cars. adj. noun verb Kaas raku•kariu tatutak•erit. I saw many cars. noun adverb verb

Tskiri Akitaru'

Pahuks•Tatu' "Pumpkin Vine" Tuhwits•Pihat . "Camp on the Ridge" Tuhwa•Hu'kasa "Shady Lane" Tskiri•Raru' "Wolf Ways" (English names given are nicknames, not necessarily strict interpretations. RESEARCH PROJECT: talk to various Skidi speakers about the interpretations of these names.)

Making Verb Phrases Into Nouns

The indicator hiri is often used to make a verb phrase into a noun, like English turns it rains into where it rains. Hiri will come first in a, verb phrase, and will usually seem to replace an indicator like ti, coming just before a weak indicator like ra. ti .ra wa•raruhat there are mountains hiri .ra wa .raruhat (to) the mountains, where the mountains are ti ratukstak•kitkeu' that's where we used to live hiri ratukstak•kitkeu' where we used to live VERB SET: Take ra'at?
