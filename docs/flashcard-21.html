Spelling

Week 21: People & Society

20 words. Click a card to flip it. “Got it” and “Missed” also tell the Practice page when to bring a word back.

Two spellings of one word. The large word on a card is in the dictionary's spelling: a long vowel written double (aa), c for both “ts” and “ch”, and ʔ for the catch in the throat. The switch at the top of the page, Spelling, puts the learner spelling there instead, with â î û for long vowels, ' for the catch, c for “ts” and č for “ch”, which is the alphabet of the Pawnee Nation's own lesson handouts. The smaller word under it is the same word in the other spelling, and the line in capitals and hyphens is a rough guide for English speakers, not a spelling. A card whose back names the textbook and no dictionary page is a word found only in the 1979 textbook: it has one spelling, the textbook's in the dictionary's letters (ʔ for '), and the switch does not change it. Where a teacher or a speaker writes a word another way, follow them.

Skiri or South Band? The dictionary's words are Skiri. A card whose back names the textbook is spelled the textbook's way, and the textbook's spelling follows South Band speakers: where it writes e, a Skiri speaker says i.
