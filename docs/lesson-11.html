KA RIiH•RATSAUS?

Lesson 11 — Are You Hungry?

Blue Book pp. 78–82

Why do the spellings differ?

The same Pawnee word appears in several writing systems, and they are all telling you the same thing:

  • Blue Book — the practical spelling printed in Pari Pakuru' (1979), exactly as the Pawnee Cultural Retention Committee wrote it. Some letters follow English habits (for example d for the soft Pawnee r).
  • Dictionary entry — the learner spelling from the Parks Dictionary, with a circumflex (â) marking long vowels.
  • Say it — the simplified pronunciation. UPPERCASE syllables carry high pitch, which changes meaning in Pawnee.
  • IPA — the exact scholarly transcription recorded by Douglas Parks from fluent speakers. When the other spellings disagree, the IPA is the authority.

Time and Subject Indicators: hiri and we Most statements in Pawnee begin with a time or subject indicator. We is used to begin almost any statement; it is a syllable which comes just before the introducer and means now or then. Really, it means at that certain time and is used to point out a time which may not be already understood by the hearer. Hiri means that thing or that place and refers to the real subject of a sentence which is known by the hearer but not necessarily understood without this word to point it out. If you are using one of the verb phrases given in this book to make a statement to somebody, you should normally start with we. Ta.tuks•at. I went We tatuks•at. I went (then) (or, I had gone) VERB SET (Various aspects): to drink Tat .kika'. I have drunk, I drank. (perfect aspect) Tat•kikahu ' . I am drinking. (imperfect aspect) Tatuks•kika'. I drank, I had drunk. (past tense, perfect aspect) Tatuks•kikahu'. I was drinking. (past tense, imperfect aspect) Suks•kika ' . Drink! (Command) The Suggestive Mode Some commands are given like suggestions, such as the comparison of "Shut the door" and "Would you shut the door?". These commands take the form of normal verb phrases with a suggestive modal indicator ke+. Tiku•'u. He gave it to me. Kesku•'u. Tasku•'u. Give it to me. You should give it to me. You gave it to me.
