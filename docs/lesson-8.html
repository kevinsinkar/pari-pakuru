KI Rid RA. KU?

Lesson 8

Blue Book pp. 65–70

Why do the spellings differ?

The same Pawnee word appears in several writing systems, and they are all telling you the same thing:

  • Blue Book — the practical spelling printed in Pari Pakuru' (1979), exactly as the Pawnee Cultural Retention Committee wrote it. Some letters follow English habits (for example d for the soft Pawnee r).
  • Dictionary entry — the learner spelling from the Parks Dictionary, with a circumflex (â) marking long vowels.
  • Say it — the simplified pronunciation. UPPERCASE syllables carry high pitch, which changes meaning in Pawnee.
  • IPA — the exact scholarly transcription recorded by Douglas Parks from fluent speakers. When the other spellings disagree, the IPA is the authority.

Dialogues

Kirike ras•taspe'? What are you looking for?
Tah•raspe ' asaki. I'm looking for my dog.
Ka ras•huras asaki? Have you found the dog?
Ahu', tat .huras asaki. Yes, I found the dog.
Kiru ruks•ku'? Where was it (sitting)?
Ru tuks•kaku'. It was in the house.

Grammar & Notes

Past Tense

There is one marker in Pawnee which refers to time. This is the marker +uks, which follows the person marker in the introducer and means in a time past. We have seen that +uks also marks the command form, but in that usage, the introducer has no marker of mode. Suks .atku. Tasuks•atku. Listen! You listened. The u in +uks is a strong vowel which causes the a in modal marker +ra and i in modal marker ti + to drop out. question mode person past verb kiru +ra +0 +uks +ku kiru +ra +uks +ku (a drops out) Niru ruks•ku? Where was it? Pawnee does not use a letter or root to refer to the third person. It's the fact that no person marker is present which means third person. The symbol is used to denote the absence of a marker in the interpretation. tat .kiikat tat. =ta + t tas .kikat tas. = to + s ti .kikat ti. = ti + I am crying first person you are crying second person he, she, it is crying third person Notice also that the modal marker has a different vowel for third person. mode person past root root ti +1 +uks +ka +ku' ti +uks +ka +ku' Tuks .kaku '. He was in the house.

Nouns In A Verb Phrase

In Pawnee, a noun can often be included inside the verb phrase, as we saw in Lesson 3. That was a case of subject nouns being included. Nouns of location can also be included. First they are reduced to a root form and then inserted before the verb. 3g hatur•raru' road ti•kaarit he is standing in it ti +hatur +ka +arit r becomes h before k +ka + "in" ti +hatuh +ka +arit ti•hattih•kaarit he's standing in the road
