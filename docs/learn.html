Learning Skiri Pawnee with this site

Seven steps, in an order we suggest. Each one is a page of this site. Tick a step when you have done it; the ticks stay in this browser.

  1. 1. Learn the sounds

    Skiri has nine consonants and three vowels, each short or long. Most are close to English. Four things are new: the letter c, the tapped r, the glottal stop ʔ, and holding a long vowel twice as long.

    Do: read the page once, then take ten words through “Sound it out”.

  2. 2. Say your first sentences

    The twenty lessons of Pari Pakuru', the 1979 textbook of the Pawnee Cultural Retention Committee. Each begins with a few words and puts them to use at once in a short dialogue: “What is it?” “This is a feather.”

    Do: lesson 1 first. Learn its basic words, read the dialogue aloud, both parts, then cover one side and say it from memory. One lesson at a time after that.

  3. 3. Use a phrase today

    “Come here.” “Wait!” “I'm full.” “Thank you.” The textbook's useful phrases, gathered by situation: at home, at the table, coming and going.

    Do: pick three and say them when the moment comes. Tomorrow keep them and add one.

  4. 4. Build up your words

    The flashcards are sets of 10 to 20 everyday words. The first three weeks are words from the textbook; the later weeks go by topic, from family to places.

    Do: one set a week. Say every word aloud before you turn the card. Print the word list (PDF).

  5. 5. See how words are built

    iksuʔ “hand” is iks + uʔ. asaaki “dog” is asaa + kis. The dictionary prints the pieces of most words. Once you can read them, a new word is often two pieces you already know.

    Do: look up the pieces of five words you have learned, and read the other words that share them.

  6. 6. Meet the verb

    A Skiri verb changes with who does the thing and how it is meant, and much of the change is at the front of the word. The dictionary gives five forms of every verb and writes seven verbs out in full.

    Do: learn “to go” in the indicative: eleven words. Then compare it with “to come”.

  7. 7. Practise a little every day

    Six short games: choose the meaning, choose the word, match pairs, type the word, verb forms and word pieces. The page remembers each word you meet and brings it back when it is due: the next day, then after three days, a week, two weeks and a month.

    Do: one round a day, ten questions. It takes about three minutes.

Before you start

This site was put together from two printed books with computer tools. No speaker of Pawnee and no one from the Pawnee Nation has reviewed it yet. It can show you how a word is written and how the books say it is built; it cannot let you hear it. Learn from speakers and teachers wherever you can, and trust them before this site.
