RI•KATIHARU'

Lesson 15 — The Year

Blue Book pp. 95–98

Why do the spellings differ?

The same Pawnee word appears in several writing systems, and they are all telling you the same thing:

  • Blue Book — the practical spelling printed in Pari Pakuru' (1979), exactly as the Pawnee Cultural Retention Committee wrote it. Some letters follow English habits (for example d for the soft Pawnee r).
  • Dictionary entry — the learner spelling from the Parks Dictionary, with a circumflex (â) marking long vowels.
  • Say it — the simplified pronunciation. UPPERCASE syllables carry high pitch, which changes meaning in Pawnee.
  • IPA — the exact scholarly transcription recorded by Douglas Parks from fluent speakers. When the other spellings disagree, the IPA is the authority.

Useful Phrases

  • Aka, hiri we ti•pitsi ' ! — Gee, it's cold in here!
  • Aka, hiri we ta•hu•tuhtu'! — Gee, it's cold in this wind!
  • Hiri we ta•hu•tawi•rihu! — It's really windy'!
  • Suks•teka .rikut. — Open the door.
  • Suks•teka•wu. — Close the door.

Grammar & Notes

Verb-Final K

No Pawnee word ends in k. If there is a k in the underlying root form of a verb, it will show up only before a verb suffix (ending) such as the habitual +usuku ' or the imperfect +us or +u'. Everywhere else, the verb final k will drop off (or, for some verbs, change to t.) tirarah-karuk•usuku' they plant (habitual) tirarah .karuku they are planting (imperfect) tirardh•karu they have planted (perfect) tirarah•kiwik•usuku ' they roast corn tirardh•kiwiku' they are roasting corn tirardh•kiwi they have roasted corn
