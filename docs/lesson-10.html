KIRIK1 RUH•RA'?

Lesson 10 — What Is He Using?

Blue Book pp. 74–78

Why do the spellings differ?

The same Pawnee word appears in several writing systems, and they are all telling you the same thing:

  • Blue Book — the practical spelling printed in Pari Pakuru' (1979), exactly as the Pawnee Cultural Retention Committee wrote it. Some letters follow English habits (for example d for the soft Pawnee r).
  • Dictionary entry — the learner spelling from the Parks Dictionary, with a circumflex (â) marking long vowels.
  • Say it — the simplified pronunciation. UPPERCASE syllables carry high pitch, which changes meaning in Pawnee.
  • IPA — the exact scholarly transcription recorded by Douglas Parks from fluent speakers. When the other spellings disagree, the IPA is the authority.

Dialogues

Paresu kirike rut•ari '?
What is the hunter doing?
Paresu ti•taka•asiku'.
The hunter is shooting.
Kirike ruh•ra' paresu '?
What is the hunter using?
Paresu tuh•ra' tiraku ' .
The hunter is using a rifle.

Dialogue lines are shown in the Blue Book's practical spelling, exactly as printed.

Useful Phrases

  • Ra kitu'. — Altogether.
  • Arisit suks•at. — Go by yourself.
  • Suks•kata .hat! — Get out of the way!
  • Suks•taka•hat! — Get off (out)!
  • Tas•kitawe' — You're the boss.

Grammar & Notes

Pre Verbs

There are many Pawnee verbs which add +ut or +ur to the introducer. +Ut can mean for him or on him and may be optional for some verbs. +Ur means for it, him, her or for that purpose. ti .ari' ti +ut +ari tut•ari ' ti .ra ti +ur +ra tur +ra tuh+ra tuh•ra' he is becoming he +for it +becomes i +u becomes u he is doing it, making it he has it he +for that (purpose) +has it i +u becomes u r+r becomes h +r he is using it
