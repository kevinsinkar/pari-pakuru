Word building

Word web: how words share their pieces

Many Skiri words are built from smaller pieces, and the dictionary prints the pieces. Here each word is a box, joined to the pieces of its own derivation. Words that share a piece gather round it, so one piece you know leads you into a whole family of words.

What this picture is, and is not

Every word, meaning and piece here is printed in the dictionary. A line says one thing only: the dictionary prints this piece in the derivation of this word. The page cuts a derivation at its plus signs and draws the result; it adds no analysis of its own, and where a shape sits means nothing.

An oval gathers the words in which the piece is printed with the same letters. The same letters are not always the same piece: the dictionary has two entries spelled kuruks, one “bear” and one “boil, carbuncle”, and short pieces such as a, ra and at are printed in a great many words. Pieces printed with different letters are kept apart, even where their words mean alike: kaatiit and katiit each stand in words for black things. The page does not say what a piece means. Read the words round it, and the literal translations under the picture, as you would in the list of words that share a piece.

Words to do with ceremony are left out until the Pawnee community has reviewed them. No speaker of Pawnee has reviewed this page.
