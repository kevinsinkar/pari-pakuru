Learn with people, and hear the language

A language is learned from people. This site is made from two books and cannot let you hear a word. These places can do what it cannot. They belong to the people who made them: this site links to them and copies nothing from them.

Learn with the Pawnee Nation

The language is the Pawnee people's, and the Nation teaches it. Begin there, and trust what you learn there before this site.

  • The Pawnee Nation's language programme The Nation's own programme, and the first place to ask. Its website announces language and culture classes; in March 2025 it announced online language classes. Ask the programme what is running now. Both Free (classes, incl. online)
  • The language department's Facebook page The Nation's website gives this page as the place to follow its language department: class news and posts. Both Free (Facebook)
  • Recordings of the Nation's language classes A playlist, “Pawnee Language Classes”, on the Pawnee Nation's own YouTube channel. Free online
  • The Nation's lesson handouts Three lessons as PDFs, provided by the Pawnee Nation and hosted by Northern Oklahoma College: the alphabet, greetings and introductions, basics. They give Skiri and South Band forms side by side, in the spelling this site calls the learner spelling. Both (side by side) Free online
  • The Nation's Skiri course A beginners' course made from 1997 by the Pawnee Nation with Indiana University: the textbook “Introduction to the Pawnee Language (Skiri Dialect)”, lessons on CD-ROM with a speaker's recordings, and a dictionary with sound. It is not online. The university's page says copies are available from the Nation's Education Department. Skiri textbook; lessons in both Ask Pawnee Nation or IIK (availability unclear)

Hear the language

This site cannot let you hear a word. These can.

  • Two people of the programme talk about the language A podcast interview of January 2022, in English, 1 hour 23 minutes, with two citizens of the Pawnee Nation who work with its language programme: how they came to the language and what the programme is working towards. It is a conversation about the language, not a lesson. Both Free online
  • Indiana University's Skiri dictionary, with sound The dictionary this site is made from, online at the Institute for Indigenous Knowledge. Search in English or in Pawnee; some entries have a recording you can play. Type a Pawnee word in plain letters, without the mark for the catch in the throat. Skiri (separate South Band database) Free online

Every dictionary word on this site also has a link, “Hear it at Indiana University”, that opens a search for the word in that dictionary. Some entries there have a recording; many do not, and a few words are not found by the search.

About this list

Each link was opened in November 2026 and says what the page was seen to say then; pages move, and classes change. The list is short on purpose. The project keeps a longer register of what has been written and recorded about Pawnee, with notes on dialect and on who holds the rights, in its files. Works on ceremony and collections of songs are not listed here: whether and how they are shared is for the Pawnee Nation to say.

Two dialects are spoken of in these sources, Skiri and South Band. This site's dictionary is Skiri; the 1979 textbook writes mostly as South Band speakers say the words. The Nation's handouts and the Alphabet Book show both side by side.
