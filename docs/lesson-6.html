Lesson 6 — Words to substitute for pita in the dialogues.

Blue Book pp. 55–60

Why do the spellings differ?

The same Pawnee word appears in several writing systems, and they are all telling you the same thing:

  • Blue Book — the practical spelling printed in Pari Pakuru' (1979), exactly as the Pawnee Cultural Retention Committee wrote it. Some letters follow English habits (for example d for the soft Pawnee r).
  • Dictionary entry — the learner spelling from the Parks Dictionary, with a circumflex (â) marking long vowels.
  • Say it — the simplified pronunciation. UPPERCASE syllables carry high pitch, which changes meaning in Pawnee.
  • IPA — the exact scholarly transcription recorded by Douglas Parks from fluent speakers. When the other spellings disagree, the IPA is the authority.

Dialogues

Pita kirike rut•ari'? What is the man doing?
Pita tiwari'. The man is going (walking) about.
Pita ka ra•kikat? Is the man crying?
Kaki ', pita t'ewasku'. No, the man is laughing.

Dialogue lines are shown in the Blue Book's practical spelling, exactly as printed.

Grammar & Notes

Pre Verbs

Some verbs in Pawnee are accompanied by markers which indicate certain relationships like those indicated by English prepositions in to it, for it, and on it. Two of these markers are + ut and + ur and are included in the introducer, which is why they are called preverbs. ti•ari' he's becoming tut•ari ' he 's making it or doing it (tu-td-ri') ti + ut + ari it is + for him + becoming

Useful Phrases

1. Siksut•tsa' You (1) stand up. 2. We rut•utsi•raru'. That's all. 3. TI'tskipi. He's sleepy. 4. Tiku 'tskipi. I'm sleepy. 5. Ta'tskipi. You're sleepy.

Verb Sets

Questions Kirike ratut•ari '? What am I doing? Kirike rasut•ari'? What are you (1) doing? Kirike rutari '? What is he doing? Kirike rit•ari '? What are they (3+) doing? ri + ut Kirike sirut•ari'? What are they (2) doing? Responses All of the following responses would normally be preceded by the indicator we which means "now " or "then." (we) Tat•pari ' . I am walking about. Tas•pari ' . You are walking about. Ti .wari'. He is walking about. Siti .wari'. They (2) are walking. Tirah•wari'. They (3+) are walking. (ti-rah-wha-ri') (we) Tat•kikat. I am crying. More responses can be built by adding the introducers tat ., taw, etc. above to the verb root + kikat.

Person Markers

In Pawnee verb phrases, the introducer will tell who is being talked about, that is, who the subject and object of the sentence are. When a single letter or root is used, we call this a person marker. There are four main person markers in Pawnee: 1. Subjects (who is doing it) + t first person, I or we (speaker or any group which includes him) tat. kikat I am crying sitat•kikat we (2) are crying + s second person, you tas•kikat you are crying sitas .kikat you (2) are crying 2. Objects (who is experiencing it) + ku first person, to me, to us tiku'tskipi I am sleepy sitiku'tskipi we (2) are sleepy tikurak•itskipi we (3+) are sleepy + a second person to you ta 'tskipi you (1) are sleepy sita'tskipi you (2) are sleepy tarak•itskipi you (3+) are sleepy 3. Third Person: he, she, it Third person refers to someone or something who is neither the speaker nor the hearer. In English, the words he, she, and it refer also to sex and the difference between a person and a thing. Pawnee makes no such distinctions. Ti pahaat means he is red or she is red or it is red.
