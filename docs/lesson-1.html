KIRIK1 RU'?

Lesson 1 — What Is It?

Blue Book pp. 29–33

Why do the spellings differ?

The same Pawnee word appears in several writing systems, and they are all telling you the same thing:

  • Blue Book — the practical spelling printed in Pari Pakuru' (1979), exactly as the Pawnee Cultural Retention Committee wrote it. Some letters follow English habits (for example d for the soft Pawnee r).
  • Dictionary entry — the learner spelling from the Parks Dictionary, with a circumflex (â) marking long vowels.
  • Say it — the simplified pronunciation. UPPERCASE syllables carry high pitch, which changes meaning in Pawnee.
  • IPA — the exact scholarly transcription recorded by Douglas Parks from fluent speakers. When the other spellings disagree, the IPA is the authority.

Dialogues

Kirike ru'? What is it?
Ti hitu '. This is a feather.
Ka rii hitu '? Is this a feather?
Ahu ', ti hitu'. (SB) Yes, this is a feather.
Hau, ti hitu'. (SK)
Ka ru karitki? Is this a rock?
Kaki, kaki karitki. No, it is not a rock.
Kirike ru'? What is it?
Ti hitu '. This is a feather.

Grammar & Notes

Nouns

There are very few short words in Pawnee. Most words are more descriptive and are longer constructions of smaller parts. The shorter words are usually names of common objects, such as the things named in this chapter. Words that name things are called nouns, and nouns in Pawnee often end in the suffix -u'. (A suffix is a small part of a word attached to the end of it.) Some nouns in Pawnee are words which have been copied or borrowed from English; when this happens, the word is given a distinct Pawnee pronunciation, and often it is hard to hear the English equivalent. Look at the names for nickel, dime, and quarter above. What English words do they suggest?

Pronunciation

At first, you may find some sounds of Pawnee hard to pronounce. One of the sounds is the letter r. It is easier to pronounce between vowels. There it sounds like a fast d as in the English word ready, but is softer. Drill: (iri) kiriks (ari) karitki (ira) tirakis When the r begins a word, it is helpful to stick in a very short a just before the r, so that you put the r between vowels. Drill: rakis (arakis) raku' (araku')

Useful Phrases

1. Nawa! Hello, okay, now. The old pronunciation of this word is rawa, but the pronunciation and spelling with n is so common, we have included this word as the only one to use n. Common expressions tend to receive unique pronunciations. Notice that the Skidi word for yes (hail) ends with a strong nasal quality (sound coming through the nose). 2. Istu. Again. 3. Suks-paku'. Say it. 4. Aka! Oh my! Gee! 5. Ka? What?

Indicators

Indicators are Pawnee words which indicate statements, questions, denials, and so on. Ka ru'? Ka ru kisu'? Ti'. Ti kisu'. Kaki'. Kaki kisu'. Is it? Is it a bone? It is. This is. This is a bone. It is not. No. It is not a bone. KIRIKJ RA•TARAWIS?
