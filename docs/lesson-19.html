Lesson 19 — The Future

Why do the spellings differ?

The same Pawnee word appears in several writing systems, and they are all telling you the same thing:

  • Blue Book — the practical spelling printed in Pari Pakuru' (1979), exactly as the Pawnee Cultural Retention Committee wrote it. Some letters follow English habits (for example d for the soft Pawnee r).
  • Dictionary entry — the learner spelling from the Parks Dictionary, with a circumflex (â) marking long vowels.
  • Say it — the simplified pronunciation. UPPERCASE syllables carry high pitch, which changes meaning in Pawnee.
  • IPA — the exact scholarly transcription recorded by Douglas Parks from fluent speakers. When the other spellings disagree, the IPA is the authority.

Dialogues

Kirike rasut•ari'sta rahesa '? What will you do tomorrow?
Rahesa ' tatut•a•wahri•usta. Tomorrow I will work.
Kiru rira•awiu' ras•uh .ta? When will you go?
Tat•uh•ta tihe ra•pa•ariki'. I will go next month.

Grammar & Notes

Future Tense

What is called in English future tense is expressed in Pawnee by the suffix +ta. It comes as the last part of a verb phrase, and means in a time yet to come. The future marker can occur as (h)ista or 'sta, the perfect (completed) aspect, or as usta, imperfect aspect. Tatutari'sta. I'll do it. (future completed) Tatut•ari .usta. I'll be doing it. (future incomplete) Tatut•a•wahri•usta. I'll be working. Months of the Year Any one of these words can be used in place of rahesa ' in the dialogues.
