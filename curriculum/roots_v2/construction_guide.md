# How Skiri Pawnee words are built

Generated by `scripts/morphology_v2/run.py --guide`. Do not edit by hand: every Pawnee form below is looked up from a source record, and `scripts/morphology_v2/check_forms.py` verifies that.

## How to read this guide

- **Sources.** Parks, A Dictionary of Skiri Pawnee (cited by entry id and printed page), his Grammatical Overview (GO) and appendices, and the Blue Book, Pari Pakuru (BB, cited by lesson and page).
- **What is shown.** Only analyses in tier A or B. Tier A: Parks prints the analysis, the pieces reproduce the headword exactly by named rules, and an independent source (the word's paradigm, or a Blue Book seam) confirms it. Tier B: Parks prints it, it reproduces exactly, and every piece recurs with the same meaning in other words. `METHOD.md` gives the rules in full.
- **Spelling.** Pawnee forms are in italics, each in the spelling of the source it is quoted from. The "Say it" column is the project's learner respelling. IPA is Parks's own phonetic form, shown only where he prints one for that exact word; it is never generated.
- **Pieces.** A hyphen after a piece marks a prefix or preverb; a hyphen before it marks a suffix. Upper-case labels (NOM, DIM, PL …) are Parks's.

**Scale.** The dictionary has 4337 words after duplicate rows are resolved. Parks prints an analysis for 3623 of them. 1093 analyses reach tier A and 1316 tier B; this guide draws on those 2409 words.

### A noun from a root: root + *-uʔ*

**Formula.** root + *-uʔ*

**Where it applies.** Nouns. The suffix adds no meaning of its own; it lets a bound root stand as a word (Parks, Grammatical Overview p. 31, Table 5).

**At the seam.** 83 of these words join with no change. In the rest: ʔ between two vowels that do not contract (20); word-initial i or a is lost before a consonant (8); word-initial w > p (3).
