# Part 1 — Source conflicts, decisions and gaps (manual)

## A. Blue Book vs Parks (and Parks vs Parks): conflicts shown, not resolved

- ⚠ 'his father': App.3 *iʔaastiʔ*; dictionary *hiʔaastiʔ* (p.40, etymology hi-+aas+riʔ); BB *hiasti'* (L7 p.62, OCR *hidsti'*). Two Parks sources disagree on the initial *h*.
- ⚠ 'my grandfather': App.3 *atipaat*; dictionary *atipat* (p.19); BB *atipat* (L7 p.60). Vowel length.
- ⚠ 'your niece/nephew': Parks *awaat* (p.20) and App.3 *awaat*; BB *swat* (L7 p.61). Different prefix (BB s- vs Parks a-).
- ⚠ 'my daughter' BB *tsuwat*, 'my son' BB *tiki'* (L7 p.61): no Parks kinship paradigm; dictionary has *tiki* 'son, sonny' only (p.165).
- ⚠ 'I have it': BB *tah•ra* (L4 p.46) vs App.1 *tahaa*; 'I don't have it' BB *Kakah•ra* vs App.1 *kaakahaa*. BB keeps the *r* that Parks's Rules 13–15 absorb.
- ⚠ 'We (3+) have them': BB *Tahraktarra* with *rak-* (L4 p.47) vs App.1 'to have it' plural with *si-* (*sitahaa*). Different plural strategy for this class-3 verb.
- ⚠ 'it is red': BB *ti pahaat* (L2 p.33) vs Parks *tipahaaʔat* (p.107; GO p.49). BB omits the reduplicated *-ʔa-* of Class I descriptives.
- ⚠ 'he is doing it': BB *tut•ari'* (L6 p.56) vs Parks 3sg perfective *tuutaa*, imperfective *tuutariʔ*. BB glosses an imperfective form as the citation form.
- ⚠ nominaliser: BB *hiri* (L13 p.88–89) vs Parks *irii-* (GO p.42). Initial *h* and vowel length.
- ⚠ commands: BB *Suks•paku'* (L5 p.53) vs Parks *i-* CONT + *s-* + *uks-* (GO p.38, *sukskirikaʔaaʔ*). BB does not write the contingent *i-*.
- ⚠ spelling rules: BB 'k drops off before p' (L3 p.41) and 's + s → s•ts' (L17 p.102) have no counterpart among Parks's 24 rules; BB 'i + a → e' corresponds to Parks Rule 7 (→ ii); BB 'w → p' is attested in 115 headwords but unnumbered in Parks.
- ⚠ BB *tsau* 'face' (L2 p.36 *tskau*) vs Parks *ckaʔuʔ*; BB *kskitsu'* 'finger' (L5 p.52) vs Parks *ikskiicuʔ*: BB drops the initial *i-* of *icka-*/*iks-* in these words; Parks does so only in *ckaʔuʔ*, *cusuʔ*, *caahiks*.
- ⚠ Parks Table 4 (GO p.30) prints the instrumental/locative suffix as *-biriʔ* (OCR); dictionary etymologies have *-hiriʔ*, *-piriʔ*, *-iriʔ*.
- ⚠ Parks GO examples containing *b* (*wiitahurabwiicaʔ*, *tuksuhuubwíitit*, *siwituksaakitaabku*, *riibiikakuspihat*) are OCR errors for *h*/*p*; cited as printed in the extraction, flagged here.

## C. Gaps in the data

- 1016 of 4343 entries have no Parks etymology; they were not segmented and count toward a root only when the headword itself is the root.
- No dictionary paradigm form and no Appendix 1 cell contains the aorist/jussive *uks-*; it is attested only in Parks's grammar examples (GO p.38) and the Blue Book (L5, L8, L13). Lesson 6.4 therefore uses BB-attested words labelled as such.
- No dictionary etymology carries the label AUG *-kusuʔ*; Parks's single example (*wiitakusuʔ*, GO p.31) is not a dictionary headword.
- The Blue Book body dialogues (*paks•ku*, *kirik•ta*, *tiku•paks•taari'*) have no dictionary headwords; parallel dictionary words (*iksta*, *kiista*, *ikskaa*) are used instead.
- The dictionary gives no form 2 for *rihuur* 'be big' and no form 3 for *kus*; compounds supply the pattern.
- Appendix 1 covers seven verbs only; mode paradigms for all other verbs are inferred, never shown, in this curriculum.
- Blue Book auto-links of type 'prefix' (404 rows in blue_book_attestations) are unreliable: e.g. BB *kisu'* 'bone' → *kisu* 'be slender' (should be *kiisuʔ*), BB *kaki'* 'no' → *ka* 'be outstanding', BB *tirakis* 'bow' → *tir* 'be together', BB *kararu'* 'stomach' → *ka*, BB *kasu'* 'leg' → *ka*, BB *atlas* 'my father' → *at* 'go'. These should be re-linked before BB forms are shown as dictionary-confirmed.
- 93 database entries come only from the Blue Book (source = blue_book) and have no Parks confirmation.
