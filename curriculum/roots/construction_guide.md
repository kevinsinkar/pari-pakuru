## 1. Two spellings of one language

Parks's dictionary and the Blue Book spell the same sounds differently. All forms in this guide and in the lessons keep the spelling of the source they are quoted from.

| Sound | Parks dictionary | Blue Book | Example (same word) |
|---|---|---|---|
| glottal stop | ʔ | ' (often dropped) | Parks *hiituʔ* — BB *hitu'* (L1 p.29) |
| affricate | c | ts | Parks *cuuraki* — BB *tsuraki* (L6 p.56) |
| long vowel | aa ii uu | usually single letter; BB p.35 asks learners to underline long syllables | Parks *piita* — BB *pita* (L4 p.44) |
| i + a contraction | ii (Rule 7) | e | Parks *tiirit* — BB *t'erit* (L8 p.66) ⚠ |
| morpheme seam | not marked (etymology uses +) | • between pieces | BB *tat•kikat* = Parks *tatkikat* |
| dialect | Skiri (SK) only | Skiri (SK) and South Band (SB) marked | BB L1 p.29 *had'* (SK) / *ahu'* (SB) 'yes' |

BB's own statement of long vowels: BB p.35: “rakis + rahkata = rakis•tahkata” shows a seam written with •; Parks writes the result as one word.

## 2. Word classes and the shape of a stem

**Nouns.** Parks GO p.29: an independent stem stands freely 'with or without the nominal absolutive suffix -uʔ'; a dependent stem (N-DEP) needs a suffix or a compound — *asaa-* 'horse; dog' → *asaaki* 'dog'. Five derivational affixes only (Table 5, GO p.31): NOM *-uʔ* (*iksuʔ*), DIM *-kis* (*asaaki*), AUG *-kusuʔ* (Parks's *wiitakusuʔ* 'large man'; no dictionary etymology carries the label ⚠), 'covering' *-kuukuʔuʔ* (*pakskuukuʔuʔ* 'hat'), FEM *c-* (Parks's *ckuraaʔuʔ* 'female doctor').

**Verb roots and stems.** Parks GO p.31: 'the basic component of active verb stems is the root ... approximately one hundred such roots', so most stems are derived. The two most productive are *hak* 'pass' and *at* 'go'. In the extraction, *at* builds 156 dictionary words and *hak* 69 (see `roots_ranked.json`). Three preverbs — *ir-/a-*, *uur-*, *ut-* — form discontinuous stems and 'do not have any tangible meaning' when part of a stem (GO p.31): *aar* 'become' 3sg *tiʔaa* vs *aar* (ut...) 'do' 3sg *tuutaa*.

## 7. Nouns: formation, case, incorporation, possession

**Formation.** Root + *-uʔ* (*iksuʔ*), root + *-kis* (*asaaki*), root + root + *-uʔ* (*ikskiicuʔ* 'finger' = hand + neck). Case and number (GO p.30, Table 4): body-part nouns take PL *-raar-* and INST/LOC *-hiriʔ* (Parks's *ikstaaririʔ* 'with the hands'); other nouns LOC *-kat* (*picikat* 'in cold weather') and INST *-hiriʔ*; names LOC *-ru* (*Kirikuurukstu* 'Anadarko').

**Incorporation** (GO p.43): body parts, natural phenomena, foods and cultural products go before the stem and drop *-uʔ*: *ikskaa* 'have one's hand in, as a glove or drawer' (3sg *tiikskaa*); Parks's *ahikiistacakipu* 'he gathered the bones' (*kiis-* + *raar-* PL + *takipuh*). Nouns that do not incorporate: personal names, place names, kinship terms, 'man/woman', animal names — with exceptions such as *tuuricaahiksii* 'he is a good-looking person' (GO p.43). Blue Book: BB p.68: “r becomes h before k” (L8 p.68, *hatur* + *ka* + *arit* → *ti•hatuh•kaarit* 'he's standing in the road').

**Possession.** Verbal: *ir...uk* (+ *ku-*) agent possessor, *ir...ri...uur-* physical possessor, *uur-* patient possession, *ut-* benefactive (GO p.36–37); Blue Book frame BB p.51: “mode + phys. poss. + person + noun + verb” (L5). Kinship (Appendix 3) is the only inflected noun class:
